# Beginner method — overview

This method solves the cube **layer by layer**, from the bottom up. It needs
**seven algorithms** in total, two of which are mirror images of each other.

## The plan

| | Step | What you get | Algorithms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [The white cross](1-white-cross.md) | A white cross underneath, sides matched | none |
| 2 | [The white corners](2-white-corners.md) | The whole bottom layer | 1 |
| 3 | [The second layer](3-second-layer.md) | Two thirds of the cube | 2 (mirrors) |
| 4 | [The yellow cross](4-yellow-cross.md) | A yellow cross on top | 1 |
| 5 | [Placing the edges](5-yellow-edges.md) | The yellow edges in their places | 1 |
| 6 | [Placing the corners](6-corners-placed.md) | The corners in the right spots | 1 |
| 7 | [Orienting the corners](7-corners-oriented.md) | **Solved cube** | 0 (reused) |

## Three pieces of advice before you start

!!! tip "Don't try to understand the algorithms"
    Steps 1 to 3 are done **by reasoning**: you see where the piece goes, you
    put it there. Steps 4 to 7 use algorithms: there, you apply them without
    asking why. Understanding comes from repeating them, not from staring at
    them.

!!! tip "Finish each step completely before moving on"
    Every step assumes the previous one is **entirely** done. An "almost right"
    white cross ruins everything that follows.

!!! danger "A cube that looks impossible"
    If you reach the end with **one single** flipped edge, or **one single**
    twisted corner, it is not your mistake: your cube was taken apart and
    reassembled the wrong way. See [Troubleshooting](../troubleshooting.md).

## The starting position

You hold the cube with the **white centre on the bottom** and the **yellow
centre on top**. These two centres will not move again until the end.
