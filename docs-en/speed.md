# Getting faster

This page contains **no algorithms**. It contains what makes the difference once
you know the algorithms — and what will save you the most seconds if you are
just starting.

## What actually slows you down

Time yourself once and watch **where the time goes**. The answer is almost always
a surprise:

| What you think | What is actually happening |
|---|---|
| "I turn too slowly" | You turn fast, but you **stop** between steps |
| "I'm missing algorithms" | You spend 40% of the time **hunting** for the next piece |
| "I need to practise more" | You are repeating your mistakes faster |

The pauses matter more than finger speed. A 60-second solve typically contains
**20 seconds of motionless hands**.

## Lookahead

This is the core skill, and it can be trained.

The idea: while your fingers execute a known sequence, **your eyes are already
hunting for the next piece**. You do not need to watch your hands, they know what
they are doing.

### The drill

Solve **very slowly**, at a third of your normal speed, forbidding yourself any
pause. The cube must turn continuously, gently but never stopping.

It is frustrating and it gives you terrible times for a week. Then the pauses
disappear, and you come back up much faster than before.

!!! tip "The 3-second rule"
    If you go more than 3 seconds without moving the cube, you are hunting —
    that is where your gain is, not in a new algorithm.

## Fingertricks

A move should be done **with one finger**, not with the wrist.

| Move | Finger | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `U` | left index | pushes the top face to the right |
| `U'` | right index | the most frequent motion on the cube |
| `R` | right ring finger | pushes upwards |
| `R'` | right index | pulls downwards |
| `F` | right index + left thumb | |
| `M` | left ring finger | pulls the slice towards you |

!!! tip "The sexy move test"
    `R U R' U'` must be done **without moving your palms**, using only your
    fingers. If you readjust your grip between moves, the fingertrick is not
    there yet. Repeat it slowly until the grip stops moving.

## The hardware

!!! warning "The original cube is a real handicap"
    The branded Rubik's cube from a shop **locks up**: it will not turn unless
    the faces are perfectly aligned. Any modern "speedcube" at €15 is radically
    nicer and removes half of your hesitations.

    It is the only purchase that makes a real difference. The rest is comfort.

A few pointers:

- **Tension**: the cube should turn without forcing, but not fall apart when you
  shake it. Most recent cubes adjust without tools.
- **Lubricant**: one drop, twice a year. Most people use far too much.
- **Magnets**: they make the faces "click" into alignment. Very pleasant, not
  essential.

## A training plan you can stick to

Twenty minutes, three times a week, beats three hours on a Sunday.

1. **5 min — warm-up.** Ten normal solves, no timer.
2. **5 min — cross only.** Twenty timed crosses, with inspection.
3. **5 min — one weak point.** A single F2L case, or three PLL algorithms.
4. **5 min — slow solves.** With no pauses at all, as described above.

!!! success "How to know whether you are improving"
    Do not look at your personal best: it depends on scramble luck. Look at your
    **average over 12 solves**, dropping the best and the worst. That is the
    measure used in competition, and the only one that moves readably.
